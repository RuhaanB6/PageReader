Setups

Eight setups plus the calibration you already shot. Document type is the least useful axis — a sheet, a letter and a book page all look like one bright quadrilateral to a detector — so the variety here is spent on surface and lighting, which is what actually broke things. Order is deliberate: failure cases first, so stopping halfway still leaves the data that matters.

  1. 00 Calibrated lightdesksheetbright

    Done — 48 frames, diversity 27. Thresholds tuned from it: the blur gate is now scene-relative rather than a fixed number.

  2. 01 Failure case lightdesksheetbright

    Same scene as calibration, now all nine takes. Light page on a light surface — the case that broke the edge detector and the colour detector both.

  3. 02 Failure case carpetnewspaperbright

    Newspaper on the patterned carpet. The exact scene that made the detector box the floor. The negative take here — carpet, no document — is the single most valuable negative in the set.

  4. 03 Failure case lightdesknewspaperdim

    Newsprint is grey, not white, and dim light shrinks every contrast further. Turn the main light off; work by a lamp or window.

  5. 04 darkdesksheetbright

    The easy baseline, and the regression control. If the trained model ever fails here, the problem is the training rather than the scene. Optional: swap in a glossy page for one take — nothing else in this list has specular glare.

  6. 05 Discrimination clutteredsheetmixed

    Keyboard, mug, phone, laptop in frame alongside the page. Teaches the model to pick the page out of other objects, not just off bare background — nothing else in this list does that.

  7. 06 woodnewspapersidelit

    Newspaper on bare wood, lit hard from one side. Strong grain under the page plus a brightness gradient across it.

  8. 07 lightdesklettershadowed

    A printed document page — denser, more uniform text than the lined sheet. Let your hand or the phone cast a hard shadow across it. Half-shadowed pages are common in real use and rare in clean datasets.

  9. 08 Hardest carpetsheetdim

    Back to the carpet, now a plain white sheet in poor light — the hardest combination here. Shoot it last, once your sweeping technique has settled.

The nine takes, in order

Same sequence inside every setup. The selector auto-advances when you stop, but starting is always an explicit press.

TakeWhat to do
full-overheadWhole page in frame, camera flat above it. Drift slowly; don't hold still.
partial-leftSlide until the page runs off the left edge, then back in.
partial-rightOff the right edge, then back.
partial-topOff the top edge, then back.
partial-bottomOff the bottom edge, then back.
distanceIn until the page overflows the frame, then far back until it's small.
tiltedAbout 30° off vertical, orbiting slowly around the page.
steepSteep, close to edge-on. Heavy perspective.
negativeRemove the document. Sweep the bare surface.

The four partial takes are the point of the exercise. A page running off the frame edge is the state the user is in while being guided — a model trained only on complete pages would fail exactly when it's needed. Give them roughly 40% of your shooting time.

Operating notes

  • You don't need the computer. Frames write to the phone. Shoot anywhere, come back, then run tools/pull-collection.sh.
  • Recording stops if the app is backgrounded or the screen locks. Don't switch away mid-take.
  • Changing any setup dropdown starts a new folder — and so does relaunching the app. A setup split across two sessions lands in two folders; that's fine, pull them all.
  • Read the rejection counters, not the kept count. Climbing blur means you're sweeping too fast. Climbing dup means you're barely moving. Both should tick over steadily.
  • Re-recording a take replaces it after a confirm press, so a fumble can't quietly pollute good frames.
  • Watch free MB on screen. Budget ~200 KB per frame; the full list is roughly 300 MB.
